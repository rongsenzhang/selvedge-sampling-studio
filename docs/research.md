# Pike Textiles and the UK sampling market

Public-source review completed on 20 November 2026. Selvedge Sampling Studio is an independent application portfolio project. The review draws on manufacturer descriptions and the University of Suffolk vacancy. Questions for company staff appear below so a future project can test the assumptions behind the prototype.

## What Pike sells

An interiors brand preparing a collection needs customers to see the pattern, feel the material and distinguish one colourway from another. Pike Textile Display Limited, based in Wisbech, designs and manufactures the physical sales tools used for that purpose. Its customers include premium textile, upholstery and wallcovering businesses. A second revenue stream covers storing rolls, cutting ordered lengths and fulfilling samples on a client's behalf. The university identifies both activities in its account of the business. [University vacancy](https://careers.uos.ac.uk/job/989445) · [Pike company profile](https://www.pike-textiles.com/about-us/) · [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/00324404)

The buyer of a sample book and the person choosing a fabric in a showroom are often different people. A national retailer can need a coordinated launch across many outlets. A smaller brand can need a short initial book run. Pike offers businesses both kinds of production and lets clients combine sample formats in one launch. Its home page carries a testimonial from Gieves & Hawkes about book production. That testimonial is historical evidence of work with the brand, with no date or current contract value stated. ## Evidence to obtain before a pilot

One published example provides a useful arithmetic check. In 2019 Pike reported approximately 66 printed A4 samples from a 10 m × 520 mm wallpaper roll. With the 210 mm side across the roll, a 297 mm feed length, no trim allowance and no spoilage, two pieces fit across and 33 full rows fit along the roll. That gives 66 pieces. The example does not specify machine settings, prove that orientation is optimal, or provide allowances for fabric books. [Pike's wallpaper example](https://www.pike-textiles.com/wallpaper-samples/)

| Prototype assumption | What the team would need to establish with Pike |
| --- | --- |
| Example briefs, stock records and historic jobs are synthetic. | Ask commercial and production staff to trace recent jobs together. Seek permission to use anonymised inputs, the accepted plan, later changes and outcomes. Keep customer identifiers outside any public repository. |
| Worked examples use illustrative costing assumptions. | Identify who supplies the fabric, how setup and repeat work are charged, which units are used, and which costs are excluded. Obtain dated rates and check a sample calculation against an estimator's result. |
| A layout model can compare candidate cut orientations. | Observe usable roll width, selvedge, cutting allowance, directional pile and pattern repeat. Ask which materials cannot rotate and how offcuts are treated. Check the suggested plan on a physical sample before production. |
| A flagged condition prompts a human decision. | Record the expert's wording, triggering conditions, exceptions, owner and review date. Test counterexamples and establish who can release a job or amend a rule. |
| Public descriptions establish a need for inventory and reporting. | Request the actual interface specification, supported events or exports, unit conventions, authentication and receipt handling. Confirm what happens when stock changes between estimate and production. |
| A portfolio walk-through provides technical evidence. | For company impact, agree a baseline and trial protocol with staff, observe completion on representative jobs, record discrepancies and report results with the sample size. |
